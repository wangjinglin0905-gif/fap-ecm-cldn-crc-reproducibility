[7] Abstract
[8] Background: Fibroblast activation protein (FAP) marks cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) in colorectal cancer (CRC). Bulk-tissue correlations between stromal markers and extracellular matrix (ECM) scores are vulnerable to score-gene overlap, tissue purity, and platform artefacts. We asked whether a FAP-marked stromal program is reproducibly associated with a collagen- and fibronectin-rich matrix state across independent cohorts and data modalities, and whether epithelial SDC4/CD44 receptors co-vary with this program.
[9] Methods: We integrated TCGA-COAD/READ bulk RNA-seq (380 barcode-reviewed primary tumours), external validation in GSE39582 (566 tumour samples), GSE132465 single-cell RNA-seq (63,689 cells; 23 patients), GSE166555 independent single-cell validation (ten patients), and CPTAC-COAD proteomics (n = 97). Primary analyses used non-overlapping scores (FAP13, matrix4, receptor2). Robustness was tested with three matched empirical null distributions (10,000 draws each), ABSOLUTE purity adjustment, CMS stratification, and an independent TCGA entry (cBioPortal pan-cancer atlas, n = 592 (sensitivity analysis, not an independent cohort)) as sensitivity. Senescence-associated covariation was evaluated with four independently constructed senescence signatures. Single-cell inferences were at the patient level with mixed-effects models.
[10] Results: FAP13 correlated strongly with matrix4 in both bulk cohorts (TCGA rho = 0.932; GSE39582 rho = 0.913) but not with receptor2 (TCGA rho = -0.079; GSE39582 rho = 0.053). The association exceeded three matched empirical null distributions (empirical P <= 9.0 x 10^-4) and survived ABSOLUTE purity adjustment (rho = 0.906) and CMS stratification. CPTAC protein data confirmed FAP-ECM covariation using a FAP-excluded ECM score (rho = 0.812) while SDC4/CD44 receptor proteins remained uncoupled (ECM-receptor rho = 0.096, P = 0.35). Patient-level single-cell analysis confirmed stromal matrix coupling (rho = 0.857, P < 0.001). The FAP-marked matrix state was enriched for senescence-associated transcriptional features across cohorts and four independent senescence signatures, with MKI67-adjusted robustness. Cox analyses were null for all matrix and senescence scores in both cohorts, indicating a tissue-state marker rather than an independent prognostic marker.
[11] Conclusions: FAP reproducibly marks a collagen- and fibronectin-rich stromal tissue state across bulk, single-cell, proteomic, and sensitivity layers. Epithelial SDC4/CD44 did not show stable co-induction with this program and remain a candidate interface pending spatial and functional validation.
[12] Keywords: fibroblast activation protein; colorectal cancer; cancer-associated fibroblasts; extracellular matrix; syndecan-4; CD44; single-cell RNA sequencing; proteomics; cellular senescence; senescence-associated secretory phenotype
[13] 1. Introduction
[14] Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with approximately 1.9 million new cases and 900,000 deaths annually [1, 2]. Although screening programs have improved early detection, the biological determinants of progression from early-stage to invasive disease are incompletely understood. The tumor microenvironment (TME), particularly cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), plays a central role in CRC progression through matrix remodeling, paracrine signaling, and immune modulation [3], and senescent fibroblast populations are increasingly recognized as drivers of matrix deposition and inflammation across cancers, including CRC [42]. Fibroblast activation protein (FAP), a type II transmembrane serine protease, is a widely used marker of activated CAFs and reactive stroma across cancer types, including CRC [4, 5].
[15] Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic studies have shown that FAP+ fibroblasts interact with SPP1+ macrophages within the CRC microenvironment in a spatially organized manner [6]. However, whether FAP-marked stroma directly regulates epithelial adhesion molecules—particularly the claudin family of tight junction proteins—remains unresolved. Claudins are epithelial tight-junction proteins with member-specific and context-dependent behavior in CRC [7, 8, 9]: CLDN1 and CLDN2 are frequently upregulated [8], whereas CLDN4 can be downregulated in a TGFβ-linked manner [9]. This heterogeneity argues against a single unified upstream regulator.
[16] We previously analyzed the FAP–claudin axis and found that an FAP-CAF score showed no consistent positive association with a unified CLDN core score—across bulk TCGA-COAD stage strata and in patient-level analysis of the independent GSE166555 cohort (ρ = −0.382, 95% CI −0.962 to 0.447; Figure 2d). Instead, the most consistent association was between the FAP stromal program and ECM components (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, FN1). These observations prompted a reformulated hypothesis: rather than regulating claudins, FAP marks an ECM-rich stromal activation program, and epithelial SDC4 (syndecan-4) and CD44 receptors constitute a candidate interface through which this program may influence epithelial adhesion in early CRC.
[17] As a clinical context for the timing of any such stromal transition, established guidelines [10] classify shallow pT1 lesions—submucosal invasion < 1,000 μm, the local "pT1a" definition—as having low metastatic potential and treatable endoscopically, whereas deeper invasion or adverse features raise the question of additional surgery.
[18] SDC4 is a transmembrane heparan sulfate proteoglycan whose extracellular domain binds collagen I/III and fibronectin, transducing ECM signals intracellularly [11]. In CRC, SDC4 is highly expressed at the invasive front and in tumor buds, with membranous polarization toward adjacent stromal cells, and SDC4 overexpression is independently associated with poor disease-free and overall survival [12]. However, the functional role of SDC4 in CRC remains unexplored [12]. CD44, a hyaluronan receptor that also binds fibronectin and osteopontin, is a well-established marker of cancer stem cells and EMT in CRC [13]. A previous study reported that FAP+/CD44-high status was not associated with metastasis [14], compatible with CD44 marking an epithelial-side program distinct from the FAP-marked stromal program.
[19] In the present study, we integrated bulk transcriptomics (TCGA-COAD/READ, GSE39582), single-cell RNA-seq (GSE132465, GSE166555), spatial transcriptomics (Qi, Valdeolivas), CPTAC proteomics to test the hypothesis that FAP marks an ECM-rich stromal program that engages epithelial SDC4/CD44 receptors. All analyses used pre-specified, non-overlapping gene scores where possible, patient-level or mixed-effects single-cell inference, and multiplicity correction; analyses followed a dated, locked analysis plan (2026-07-27) rather than a pre-registration.
[20] 2. Materials and Methods
[21] 2.1 Study design, reporting framework and ethics
[22] This was a secondary analysis of publicly available, de-identified human CRC datasets. No participant was recruited, no intervention was performed, and no new human or animal data were collected. The analysis was organized into bulk molecular context, single-cell compartment localization, patient-level validation, spatial neighborhood analysis, and proteomic validation. Analyses followed a dated, locked analysis plan (2026-07-27).
[23] 2.2 Data sources and analysis units
[24] TCGA-COAD/READ bulk RNA-seq. Gene-level expression was obtained from the UCSC Xena browser (TCGA-COAD/READ, HiSeqV2). After filtering for tumor samples with complete staging and survival data, 89 tumors were retained for stage-stratified analysis (Stage I: n = 44; Stages II–IV: n = 45); 434 profiles were used for sensitivity analyses. Stage classification followed the AJCC pathologic stage as recorded in TCGA clinical annotations, which span editions 5–7; T category was treated as an ordered anatomical variable. The analytical unit was the tumor sample unless stated otherwise.
[25] GSE39582 external validation cohort. An independent CRC cohort (n = 585 arrays from 566 patients; Affymetrix HG-U133 Plus 2.0, GPL570) was downloaded from GEO on 2026-08-02 [15]. Nineteen samples annotated as "Non Tumoral" mucosa were excluded, leaving 566 tumor samples. Clinical annotations (OS event/time, RFS event/time, AJCC TNM stage, age, sex, mismatch-repair (MMR) status) were extracted from the series matrix phenotype fields. For gene-set scoring on this platform, probe-level expression was mapped to gene symbols with the hgu133plus2.db annotation (multiple probes per gene averaged).
[26] GSE132465 single-cell RNA-seq. Processed scRNA-seq data from 23 CRC patients were obtained from Lee et al. [16]. The complete cell annotation (63,689 cells) and raw UMI matrix were re-analyzed; tumor fibroblast-lineage cells (Myofibroblasts, Stromal 1–3; 1,501 cells) and tumor epithelial cells (17,469 cells) were defined from source annotations.
[27] GSE166555 independent single-cell validation. A separate CRC single-cell dataset (ten patients meeting prespecified thresholds of ≥ 20 CAF-like and ≥ 100 epithelial cells) was downloaded from GEO and processed with the same pipeline [17].
[28] Spatial transcriptomics. Two datasets were used: Qi et al. supplementary spatial files (10,004 Visium spots from three patients) [6] and the Valdeolivas et al. dataset (14 sections from seven patients) [18], both processed from raw counts where available.
[29] CPTAC-COAD proteomics. Protein quantification data were retrieved from the cBioPortal coad_cptac_2019 study [19, 20, 21]. FAP, COL1A1, COL1A2, FN1, SDC4, CD44, CLDN1, and CLDN4 protein abundance were extracted (n = 97 tumors for the first six proteins).
[31] 2.3 Gene-set scoring and the circularity problem
[32] All scores were computed as the cohort-level mean of within-cohort z-scored gene expression (z-score mean). Three primary, mutually non-overlapping scores were used: FAP13 (FAP, POSTN, THY1, PDPN, TAGLN, ACTA2, MMP2, MMP9, CXCL12, TGFB1, INHBA, WNT2, WNT5A), matrix4 (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, FN1), and receptor2 (SDC4, CD44). Because the original 17-gene FAP-CAF and 6-gene ECM-SDC4/CD44 scores shared four matrix genes, correlations between them are structurally inflated; the overlapping-score results are reported only as descriptive sensitivity analyses. An ESTIMATE-style 80-gene stromal signature (collagens, ECM components, proteases, TIMPs, LOX family, integrins, and myofibroblast markers) was used for stromal-content scoring in tumour–stroma ratio (TSR)-proxy and nomogram analyses [34]. Senescence-associated scores were computed as within-cohort z-score means of the SenMayo panel (125 genes; Molecular Signatures Database accession M45803; Saul et al. [37]) and of a 25-gene SASP core set (IL6, CXCL8, IL1A, IL1B, CCL2, CCL5, CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL3, CXCL10, MMP1, MMP3, MMP9, MMP10, MMP13, SERPINE1, PLAU, TIMP2, VEGFA, GDF15, IGFBP3, TNF, CSF2, HGF, FAS). To address the red-line concern that senescence readouts are confounded by proliferation, FAP13–SenMayo associations were recomputed as partial Spearman correlations on MKI67 rank residuals [37]; as a senescence-marker check, CDKN2A and CDKN2B expression was compared between FAP13-high and FAP13-low tumours (median split) by Wilcoxon test. Robustness of the senescence association was evaluated with two additional, independently constructed senescence-related signatures—the CellAge senescence-inducing gene set (n = 370; Avelar et al. [43]) and a colon-fibroblast SASP core set (GDF15, MMP3, CXCL8, CXCL1, CXCL5, STC1, CCL5) [44]—and across an independent TCGA entry (cBioPortal pan-cancer atlas, n = 592 (sensitivity analysis, not an independent cohort); accessed 2026-08-07) [19]. Senescence-related correlations were analysed as a prespecified exploratory family; nominal P values are reported for these hypothesis-generating analyses, and no single test within the family carries independent inferential weight.
[33] 2.4 Bulk statistical analysis
[34] Correlations were computed with Spearman's rank method (exact = FALSE). To test whether the primary FAP13–matrix4 association exceeded transcriptomic background, three matched empirical null distributions were constructed with 10,000 draws each: random FAP13-like versus random matrix4-like scores, fixed FAP13 versus random matrix4-like scores, and random FAP13-like versus fixed matrix4. For each target gene, 500 candidate genes were selected by proximity in mean expression, log standard deviation and detection rate across 380 barcode-reviewed TCGA tumours; genes were sampled without replacement within each draw, and two-sided empirical P values used (1 + number of absolute null correlations at least as large as observed)/(10,000 + 1). For purity sensitivity, FAP13, matrix4 and ABSOLUTE purity were rank-transformed; FAP13 and matrix4 ranks were residualised separately against purity rank and project, and their residual correlation was bootstrapped (5,000 resamples) [35]. Ordinal T-category trends were assessed with patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals (5,000 resamples) and Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) correction. Survival analyses used log-rank and Cox proportional hazards regression with Schoenfeld residual checks. Lymph node metastasis was modeled by logistic regression with AUC (ROC); a nomogram was built with the rms package (T category + StromalScore) with 200-bootstrap calibration. Each bulk cohort was analyzed within-cohort only; no cross-cohort merging was performed, so no batch-effect correction was required for bulk data.
[35] 2.5 Single-cell analysis
[36] Patient-level inference (primary). To avoid pseudoreplication, the patient was the statistical unit. Raw UMI counts were normalized per cell to log1p(counts per 10,000); target-gene expression was z-scored across cells of the relevant compartment. Per-patient mean expression was summarized separately in tumor fibroblast-lineage and tumor epithelial compartments. Patients with ≥ 20 cells per compartment entered inference (15 patients); Spearman correlations were BH-corrected across the four prespecified comparisons.
[37] Mixed-effects modeling. To use all cell-level information while accounting for within-patient correlation, linear mixed-effects models (lmer, lme4) with patient random intercepts were fitted: score ~ FAP + (1|Patient). Cell-level z-scored expression was used; slopes, t statistics, and t-derived P values (df = n − 2) are reported.
[38] Cell-level differential expression (descriptive). Stromal cells were classified as FAP+ (FAP > 0) or FAP−, and differential expression was assessed with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (Bonferroni-corrected). Cell-level P values pseudoreplicate patients and are reported for effect-size ranking only.
[39] 2.6 Ligand–receptor inference
[40] Ligand–receptor interactions were inferred with CellChat (v2.2.0) on the population-weighted network, with FAP-high myofibroblasts as sender and tumor epithelial cells as receiver, including spatially constrained CellChat on the Valdeolivas Visium sections [22, 23]. Results are hypothesis-generating: they derive from cell-level co-expression and have not been reproduced at the patient level.
[41] 2.7 Fibroblast-lineage-restricted state ordering
[42] Analysis was restricted to tumor fibroblast-lineage cells (1,501 cells, 23 patients). Principal-curve ordering was rooted by a quiescence score (PI16, COL14A1, CFD, DCN, DPT, C7) that does not include FAP, matrix, or receptor genes. Within-patient correlations of the ordering coordinate with activation, matrix4, and receptor2 scores were computed; medians were tested by Wilcoxon signed-rank test with BH correction. Results are reported as state ordering, not as a temporal trajectory.
[43] 2.8 Individual claudin analysis
[44] Ten CLDN members (CLDN1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 12, 15, 18, 23) were analyzed individually within stage strata; BH correction was applied across the 20-test family (10 genes × 2 strata).
[45] 2.9 CMS, MMR, and left/right colon stratification
[46] Consensus molecular subtype (CMS) labels were recomputed in 383 tumors with CMSclassifier v1.0.0 [24] using both random-forest (RF; posterior ≥ 0.5) and single-sample predictor (SSP; correlation ≥ 0.15, delta ≥ 0.06) methods. Because 11 RF and 10 SSP classifier input genes overlapped FAP13 or matrix4, both classifiers were re-run after omitting those genes. Within-CMS correlations used 5,000 bootstrap resamples with BH correction within classifier; global interaction models used ranked matrix4 as outcome, ranked FAP13, CMS, project, and the FAP13-by-CMS interaction, with significance estimated by 20,000 project-blocked Freedman–Lane permutations and BH correction across the four full/omitted RF/SSP tests [36]. Mismatch repair (MMR) status was parsed from GSE39582 (mmr.status; 536 tumors: 77 deficient MMR (dMMR), 459 proficient MMR (pMMR)). Left/right colon was defined anatomically in TCGA (right = cecum, ascending, hepatic flexure, transverse; left = splenic flexure, descending, sigmoid).
[47] 2.10 Proteomic analysis
[48] CPTAC protein values were extracted from cBioPortal. An ECM protein score (matrix3) was the row mean of z-scored COL1A1, COL1A2, and FN1 (FAP excluded to avoid circularity); a receptor protein score was the row mean of z-scored SDC4 and CD44; a receptor protein score was the row mean of z-scored SDC4 and CD44. Spearman correlations and BH correction were applied.
[49] 2.11 Immune and stromal deconvolution
[50] MCP-counter (v1.2.0) and EPIC (v1.1.7) were applied to TCGA tumor profiles [25, 26]. An ESTIMATE-style 80-gene StromalScore was computed as the z-score mean of the stromal signature, following the rationale of the ESTIMATE method [34].
[51] 2.12 Statistical analysis and reproducibility
[52] Analyses were performed in R v4.6.1 and Python 3.13. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered significant; multiple testing was controlled by BH FDR or Bonferroni correction as specified. Effect sizes (Spearman ρ, hazard ratios, odds ratios) are reported with 95% confidence intervals where available. Multiple-testing families were defined by one biological question within one dataset. Cells and spots were not treated as independent biological replicates when patient-level inference was required.
[53] 2.13 Data and code availability
[54] TCGA-COAD data are available through UCSC Xena and the GDC Data Portal (accessed 2026-08-02); GSE132465, GSE166555, and GSE39582 through GEO (accessed 2026-08-02); CPTAC through cBioPortal (accessed 2026-08-03). Analysis scripts (R/Python), derived data, and source tables are available at [GitHub repository to be added] and [Zenodo DOI to be added].
[55] 3. Results
[56] 3.1 Non-overlapping scoring shows that matrix coupling, not receptor co-induction, is intrinsic to the FAP-marked stromal tissue state
[57] Because the original 17-gene FAP-CAF and 6-gene ECM-SDC4/CD44 scores shared four matrix genes, their correlation is structurally inflated. We therefore recomputed all primary analyses with three non-overlapping scores (FAP13, matrix4, receptor2; Table 1). In TCGA-COAD, FAP13 correlated strongly with matrix4 (ρ = 0.932) but not with receptor2 (ρ = −0.079, P = 0.464). The identical pattern was reproduced in GSE39582 (FAP13–matrix4 ρ = 0.913; FAP13–receptor2 ρ = 0.053, P = 0.209). Thus, matrix coupling is a robust, cross-cohort feature of the FAP-marked program, whereas SDC4/CD44 expression in bulk tumor tissue does not track the program—consistent with SDC4/CD44 acting as an epithelial-side interface rather than as a component of the stromal transcriptional program itself.
[58] In the unpaired TCGA comparison, tumors had higher median expression of FAP, CLDN1, and CLDN2 than adjacent-normal profiles, whereas CLDN4 was modestly lower (median differences 4.20, 5.00, 6.55, and −0.30 log2 units; all BH-adjusted P < 0.001; Figure 1a). In 32 matched tumor-normal pairs, FAP, CLDN1, and CLDN2 remained higher, whereas the paired CLDN4 difference was small and not significant (median −0.094; FDR = 0.830). The FAP-CAF score showed no stable association with the CLDN core score in either cohort (TCGA Stage I ρ = −0.071, P = 0.63; Stages II–IV ρ = 0.305, P = 0.015; GSE39582 Stage I ρ = 0.317, P = 0.053; Stages II–IV ρ = −0.090, P = 0.036). The associations that reached nominal significance were weak (|ρ| ≤ 0.32) and directionally inconsistent across cohorts and stage strata.
[59] The CMS single-sample predictor assigned 314 of 383 tumors; FAP-CAF scores differed across subtypes (Kruskal–Wallis P = 1.51×10⁻²⁸) and were highest in CMS4 (median 1.126; Figure 1d). The FAP–CMS4 association is consistent with previous CRC studies [6, 27] and is presented as context rather than a new subtype marker; FAP is a component of the CMS4 stromal signature, introducing a definitional circularity that we acknowledge. To test whether the FAP13–matrix4 association was confined to CMS4, within-CMS correlations were computed with both RF and SSP classifiers: estimates ranged from 0.745 to 0.945 (full classifiers) and from 0.787 to 0.942 after classifier-overlap gene omission (Supplementary Figure S7). Global interaction tests were non-significant after BH correction across the four full/omitted RF/SSP families (raw RF interaction P = 0.042; FDR = 0.168), indicating that matrix covariation is present across CMS classes rather than being a CMS4-specific phenomenon.
[60] The FAP13–matrix4 coupling was robust across anatomical sites (left colon ρ = 0.922, n = 32; right colon ρ = 0.949, n = 54; both P < 0.001), indicating that the core finding is not confounded by tumor location. Receptor correlations were weak and non-significant in both sites (left ρ = 0.186, P = 0.31; right ρ = −0.204, P = 0.14).
[61] 3.2 Matrix enrichment increases gradually with invasion depth; no discrete molecular switch is detectable
[62] To approximate the anatomical window of early invasion, we tested ordinal trends across T1–T4 with patient-level bootstrap confidence intervals and BH correction (Table 2). FAP expression (ρ = 0.336, 95% bootstrap CI 0.133–0.521, FDR = 0.008), FAP13 (ρ = 0.290, 95% CI 0.085–0.472, FDR = 0.016), and matrix4 (ρ = 0.280, 95% CI 0.082–0.467, FDR = 0.016) each increased modestly with T category, whereas receptor2 did not (ρ = 0.001, 95% CI −0.217 to 0.220, FDR = 0.995). These data support progressive stromal/matrix enrichment with invasion depth. They do not support a discrete molecular switch between shallow and deep pT1 or between pT1 and pT2, and the T1 subgroup (n = 5) is too small for subgroup-specific claims. The pointwise FAP–CLDN gradient observed in TCGA (T1 ρ = −0.800 → T4 ρ = +0.188) was directionally opposite in GSE39582 (T1 +0.119 → T4 −0.304); we report both gradients explicitly rather than selecting the concordant one. (Supplementary Figure S1)
[63] 3.3 External validation in GSE39582
[64] The FAP13–matrix4 coupling was reproduced in GSE39582 (ρ = 0.913; T1–2 ρ = 0.900; T3–4 ρ = 0.908; all P < 0.001). In contrast, FAP–CLDN associations were weak and directionally inconsistent across stage strata (Stage I ρ = 0.317, P = 0.053; Stages II–IV ρ = −0.090, P = 0.036) and across T strata (T1–2 ρ = 0.190, P = 0.14; T3–4 ρ = −0.081, P = 0.07). The two cohorts agree on the absence of a stable FAP–claudin association but not on any stage-dependent gradient.
[65] FAP-CAF scoring was not an independent prognostic factor in either cohort. In TCGA-COAD, multivariable Cox regression found no independent association of the FAP-CAF score with OS (HR = 1.41, 95% CI 0.74–2.69, P = 0.30); stage remained dominant (Stage II–IV vs I: HR = 5.69, P = 0.006). In GSE39582, FAP13 was not an independent predictor of OS (HR = 1.08, 95% CI 0.89–1.31, P = 0.449); T-stage group retained a strong survival association in both cohorts (TCGA HR = 6.45, P = 0.0008; GSE39582 HR = 2.22, P = 0.017). (Supplementary Figure S2)
[66] 3.4 Single-cell analysis confirms stromal matrix coupling and shows cross-compartment trends with epithelial SDC4/CD44
[67] Harmony integration of GSE132465 and the Qi dataset separated broad epithelial, stromal, myeloid, T-cell, and B-cell compartments across 117,792 cells (Figure 2a). In GSE132465 tumors, myofibroblasts had the strongest FAP, COL1A1, COL1A2, and FN1 expression; CMS-labeled epithelial populations had high EPCAM and CLDN4 with lower CLDN1/CLDN2 detection (Figure 2b).
[68] Using the patient as the statistical unit (15 patients with ≥ 20 cells per compartment; Table 3), stromal FAP correlated strongly with stromal matrix4 (ρ = 0.857, P < 0.001), confirming the bulk-level coupling at single-cell resolution. Stromal FAP showed a positive but non-significant trend with epithelial receptor2 (ρ = 0.496, P = 0.060), as did stromal matrix4 (ρ = 0.443, P = 0.098), whereas stromal FAP was negatively correlated with stromal receptor2 (ρ = −0.711, P = 0.003, FDR = 0.006). The cross-compartment trends are suggestive but do not reach significance with 15 patients; the intra-fibroblast negative correlation indicates that SDC4/CD44 are not co-induced within FAP-marked fibroblasts.
[69] Mixed-effects modeling of 1,498 fibroblast-lineage cells nested in 23 patients showed that matrix4 was strongly coupled to FAP at the cell level (slope = 0.112, t = 7.06, P < 0.001). Receptor2 showed a weak positive cell-level association with FAP (slope = 0.051, t = 2.38, P = 0.017), which contrasts with the negative patient-level correlation (ρ = −0.271, P = 0.33). This layer-of-analysis difference—weak positive within-patient cell-level co-expression versus null-to-negative between-patient correlation—is consistent with within-patient compositional confounding and does not support receptor co-induction with FAP as a robust biological signal; both effect sizes are small.
[70] In the GSE166555 independent validation cohort, CAF-like FAP was higher in tumor than normal tissue (median paired difference 2.264 log CPM; FDR = 0.014; n = 8). Epithelial CLDN1/CLDN2 were higher and CLDN4 lower, agreeing with TCGA member-specific directions (Figure 2c). Among the ten patients meeting prespecified thresholds, the CAF reduced FAP-CAF score was not positively associated with the epithelial CLDN core (ρ = −0.382; P = 0.276; FDR = 0.750; Figure 2d).
[71] Cell-level differential expression between FAP+ (n = 1,369) and FAP− (n = 4,564) stromal cells was dominated by ECM production genes (COMP log2FC = 4.81; COL1A1 2.43; COL3A1 2.10; COL1A2 1.94; FN1 1.46; Table 4). SDC4/CD44 showed small effects (log2FC = 0.39/0.29); because cell-level tests pseudoreplicate patients and patient-level receptor correlations were null or negative, we do not interpret these as receptor induction within FAP+ stromal cells. (Supplementary Figures S3 and S4)
[72] 3.5 Ligand–receptor inference and spatial analysis
[73] Exploratory CellChat identified ECM–SDC4/CD44-related pairs with FAP-high myofibroblasts as predominant sender and tumor epithelial cells as receiver (Figure 3). In the full pooled analysis, 80 FAP-high myofibroblast-to-epithelial pairs met nominal P < 0.05; the six prespecified collagen/fibronectin–SDC4/CD44 pairs were among the strongest. Spatially constrained CellChat reproduced the SDC4 pairs most consistently across Valdeolivas sections (COL1A1-SDC4, COL1A2-SDC4, FN1-SDC4 in both sections of all six patients; Figure 4). These results are hypothesis-generating and do not establish physical interaction, directionality, or patient-level reproducibility.
[74] FAP-rich regions were spatially organized in the Qi cohort (FAP-positive CAF/SPP1-macrophage co-enriched habitats 17.0–19.3% of spots; Moran's I = 0.461–0.653, P = 0.001), but positive FAP–CLDN co-patterning was not supported: the independent Valdeolivas bivariate Moran statistic was negative or null (adjusted median −0.026, P = 0.078), and the prespecified Visium proximity analysis did not show receptor-specific spatial closure (SDC4/CD44 distance enrichment 0.998, P = 0.818).
[75] 3.6 CPTAC proteomic validation: FAP–ECM coupling with receptor uncoupling
[76] CPTAC protein data (n = 97 tumors) confirmed the FAP–ECM covariation at the protein level (FAP–COL1A1 ρ = 0.720; FAP–COL1A2 ρ = 0.729; FAP–FN1 ρ = 0.814; all FDR < 10⁻¹⁵; FAP–ECM protein score ρ = 0.891, P < 10⁻³³; Table 5). A stricter, FAP-excluded matrix3 protein score (COL1A1, COL1A2, FN1) reproduced the association in an independent CPTAC analysis (ρ = 0.812, 95% CI 0.706–0.879; FDR = 1.3 × 10⁻²³; Figure 9A), confirming that the protein-level covariation does not depend on including FAP itself within the matrix score. In marked contrast, the receptor proteins did not track the program: FAP–SDC4 ρ = −0.104 (P = 0.31), FAP–CD44 ρ = 0.215 (P = 0.034; FDR = 0.077), ECM–receptor2 protein ρ = 0.102 (P = 0.32), and SDC4–CD44 ρ = −0.256 (P = 0.011; FDR = 0.051). FAP-high versus FAP-low tumors did not differ in SDC4, CD44, or the receptor score (all P ≥ 0.10) but differed strongly in the ECM score (P < 0.001). ECM–CLDN4 protein was null (ρ = −0.179, P = 0.13; n = 71). These data provide protein-level support for the receptor-uncoupling model: the matrix program is a coordinated FAP–ECM axis, while SDC4/CD44 are not co-induced with it.
[79] 3.7 A transcriptomic stromal signature, but not a 6-gene ECM proxy, is independently associated with lymph node metastasis
[80] We asked whether transcriptomic stromal-content measures could reproduce the nodal association of pathological tumor–stroma ratio (TSR) [33]. A 6-gene ECM proxy (the original ECM-SDC4/CD44 score) showed only a non-significant trend in TCGA (35.6% vs 25.0%; OR = 1.66, P = 0.36; Figure 5) and no association in GSE39582 (43.8% vs 42.2%; OR = 1.07, P = 0.73; Figure 5). In contrast, an ESTIMATE-style 80-gene StromalScore [34] was independently associated with lymph node metastasis in TCGA after T-stage adjustment (StromalScore-high vs low OR = 3.29, 95% CI 1.14–9.97, P = 0.02; multivariable OR = 3.37, P = 0.04), with an AUC of 0.687. In GSE39582, the StromalScore showed no association (OR = 1.16, P = 0.43), which we attribute to limited probe coverage of the 80-gene signature on the microarray platform (14 of 80 genes available). A Nomogram combining T category and StromalScore achieved an AUC of 0.888 (T alone 0.862; DeLong P = 0.21) with acceptable calibration (200-bootstrap mean absolute error = 0.023) (Figure 6; Table 7). We interpret these results as: (i) the 6-gene proxy underestimated the predictive value of transcriptomic stromal content; (ii) a comprehensive stromal signature partially recovers the nodal association; and (iii) pathological TSR, which integrates cellular density and architecture, remains the strongest and clinically preferred measure.
[81] 3.8 FAP–matrix co-activation is conserved across mismatch-repair subtypes
[82] Among 536 GSE39582 samples with MMR calls (77 dMMR, 459 pMMR), dMMR tumors carried higher FAP-CAF scores (median +0.293 vs −0.118, P = 1.7×10⁻⁴) and matrix/receptor composite scores (median +0.239 vs −0.003, P = 0.0012), and lower CLDN core scores (median −0.319 vs +0.073, P = 4.6×10⁻⁷) than pMMR tumors (Figure 7a). The FAP–ECM correlation was comparably strong in both groups (dMMR ρ = 0.887; pMMR ρ = 0.917; Fisher z P = 0.20), and the FAP–CLDN correlation was near zero in both (ρ = 0.012 and −0.044; difference P = 0.65), indicating that matrix co-activation does not depend on MMR subtype (Figure 7b). dMMR tumors had a lower lymph node metastasis rate (35.1% vs 48.5%, Fisher P = 0.035) but no T-stage association (P = 0.45). OS did not differ between MMR groups (log-rank P = 0.47; HR = 1.19), limited by few dMMR events (19 of 73) and the mixed adjuvant-treatment context in which dMMR tumors derive limited benefit from 5-FU-based chemotherapy [28].
[83] 3.9 Individual claudin members and secondary analyses
[84] Across 10 claudin genes and two stage strata, no member exceeded |ρ| > 0.3 and none remained significant after BH correction (Table 6). MCP-counter and EPIC placed FAP-high tumors in a stromal/myeloid-rich context rather than a simple immune desert (Figure 8). The TGFβ-RNA score correlated with SMAD3/SMAD4 reverse-phase protein array (RPPA) abundance and inversely with E-cadherin, supporting internal consistency without establishing a FAP-to-epithelial signaling direction. NicheNet, PROGENy, and DoRothEA yielded no corrected positive signals and are reported as null exploratory analyses. CDH1 mRNA showed no monotone T-category trend in either transcriptomic cohort (TCGA ρ = −0.070, P = 0.52; GSE39582 ρ = −0.058, P = 0.18) (Supplementary Figures S5 and S6).
[3.10] 3.10 The FAP13–matrix4 association exceeds matched transcriptomic backgrounds and is robust to purity and CMS stratification
[3.10a] In 380 barcode-reviewed TCGA-COAD/READ primary tumours, the FAP13–matrix4 correlation was 0.930 (95% CI 0.910–0.943; FDR = 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁶⁵), reproduced on a different platform in GSE39582 (ρ = 0.913, 95% CI 0.895–0.928; FDR = 5.8 × 10⁻²²²). Three matched empirical null distributions (10,000 draws each; candidate genes matched on mean expression, log standard deviation and detection rate) had 97.5th percentiles of 0.703 (random-versus-random), 0.839 (fixed FAP13 versus random matrix4) and 0.832 (random FAP13 versus fixed matrix4); the observed 0.930 exceeded all three distributions (two-sided empirical P from 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ to 9.0 × 10⁻⁴; Figure 9B–D). The association was thus not reproduced by gene sets matched only on abundance, variability and detection.
[3.10b] The association was robust to anatomical site (COAD ρ = 0.939, 95% CI 0.919–0.953, n = 285; READ ρ = 0.899, 95% CI 0.836–0.935, n = 94), to sequential gene and sample omission, and to adjustment for ABSOLUTE purity and project (rank-residual ρ = 0.906, 95% CI 0.879–0.924, n = 351; Figure 9A). These sensitivity analyses indicate that the FAP13–matrix4 covariation reflects a coordinated stromal matrix state rather than score overlap, a single anatomical site, or variable tumour purity.
[3.11] 3.11 The FAP-marked stromal program is enriched for senescence-associated secretory features
[3.11a] Cellular senescence is a state of stable growth arrest accompanied by a secretory program (the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, SASP) that can remodel the tumour microenvironment [37, 38], and senescence has been shown to define a distinct myofibroblast subset with immunosuppressive and matrix-modulatory functions in solid tumours [39, 40]. Because the FAP-marked program was ECM-dominated and CAF-derived, we asked whether it is enriched for senescence-associated transcriptional features. Using the 125-gene SenMayo panel (120 after removal of four genes overlapping FAP13) (Molecular Signatures Database accession M45803) [37] and a 25-gene SASP core set (Methods 2.3), the SenMayo score correlated strongly with FAP13 in both bulk cohorts (TCGA ρ = 0.848, P < 0.001; GSE39582 ρ = 0.770, P < 0.001; Figure 10A) and with matrix4 (TCGA ρ = 0.751; GSE39582 ρ = 0.630), but not with receptor2 in TCGA (ρ = 0.042, P = 0.41; GSE39582 ρ = 0.135, P = 0.001; Figure 10A). Because proliferation confounds many senescence readouts, the FAP13–SenMayo association was recomputed after adjusting for MKI67: the correlation was essentially unchanged (TCGA ρ = 0.848; GSE39582 ρ = 0.759), indicating that the association is not an artefact of proliferation-coupled gene expression (Figure 10B). Consistent with a senescence signature, CDKN2A (P = 6.9 × 10⁻⁴) and CDKN2B (P < 0.001) were higher in FAP13-high than FAP13-low TCGA tumours. The senescence enrichment was replicated in an independent TCGA entry (cBioPortal pan-cancer atlas, n = 592 (sensitivity analysis, not an independent cohort)): SenMayo–FAP13 ρ = 0.756, P < 0.001, essentially unchanged after MKI67 adjustment (ρ = 0.747). The association was also robust to two additional, independently constructed senescence-related signatures: the CellAge inducing-gene score (TCGA ρ = 0.835; GSE39582 ρ = 0.591, corrected for full 370-gene coverage) and a colon-fibroblast SASP core score (TCGA ρ = 0.420, GSE39582 ρ = 0.451; all P < 0.001), together indicating a reproducible senescence-associated transcriptional program rather than a SenMayo-specific artefact (Methods 2.3).
[3.11b] We next tested the SASP-decoupling hypothesis: if the senescence-associated secretome drives ECM deposition by FAP+ CAFs, SASP features should co-vary with matrix scores but remain uncoupled from epithelial receptor scores. SASP correlated with matrix4 in both cohorts (TCGA ρ = 0.570; GSE39582 ρ = 0.511; Figure 10A) but was uncoupled from receptor2 in TCGA (ρ = 0.036, P = 0.49). At the protein level in CPTAC (n = 97), FAP–ECM covariation was strong (ρ = 0.812 for the FAP-excluded matrix3 score) while FAP–receptor (ρ = 0.099, P = 0.34) and ECM–receptor (ρ = 0.096, P = 0.35) proteins were uncoupled (Figure 10C). The one exception was GSE39582, where SASP showed a weak positive correlation with receptor2 (ρ = 0.154, P = 2.3 × 10⁻⁴); we report this discrepancy explicitly (Section 4.4) and note that it does not reverse the null-to-negative receptor pattern in TCGA, CPTAC, single-cell, and spatial layers.
[3.11c] Together, these data indicate that the FAP-marked matrix program is enriched for senescence-associated secretory features across two independent bulk cohorts, with protein-level support for SASP–ECM covariation and receptor uncoupling. They link the previously descriptive matrix program to a senescence-related mechanism and motivate senolytic-informed strategies as candidate upstream interventions, while acknowledging that transcriptional senescence scores are not equivalent to demonstrated senescent cells (Section 4.6).

[85] 4. Discussion
[86] In this multi-cohort bioinformatics study, the most robust and reproducible finding is that FAP marks a stromal matrix-deposition program that is conserved across cohorts, disease stages, T categories, mismatch-repair subtypes, and anatomical sites, and across transcriptomic, proteomic, single-cell, and spatial layers. We addressed three methodological concerns through the following design choices: scoring circularity was removed (non-overlapping FAP13/matrix4/receptor2), single-cell inference was moved to the patient level and to mixed-effects models, and trajectory analysis was restricted to the fibroblast lineage.
[87] 4.1 FAP as a marker of the matrix program, not a claudin regulator
[88] The absence of a stable FAP–claudin association across two bulk cohorts, the failure of any individual claudin member to survive multiple-testing correction, the directional inconsistency of stage-stratified gradients, and the null protein and spatial results together reduce support for a unified transcriptional influence of FAP-marked stroma on claudins. Claudin dysregulation in CRC is more parsimoniously attributed to epithelial-intrinsic programs—CLDN1/CLDN2 upregulation [8], TGFβ-linked CLDN4 suppression [9], and oncogenic claudin-1 functions documented in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma [29]—than to stromal control. The FAP–CMS4 association is consistent with prior work [6, 27] and is not presented as a new subtype marker, given the definitional circularity of FAP within the CMS4 stromal signature.
[89] 4.2 The ECM–SDC4/CD44 interface as a candidate, spatially defined signaling route
[90] The convergence of five observations—bulk FAP13–receptor2 null correlations, matched-null and purity-adjusted robustness of the matrix association (Section 3.10), decreasing fibroblast receptor expression along the activation ordering, CPTAC receptor-protein uncoupling, and positive patient-level trends between stromal FAP/matrix and epithelial receptor2—suggests a specific, testable geometry: FAP+ CAFs deposit collagen/fibronectin matrix, and epithelial cells sense it through SDC4/CD44, without the receptors being part of the stromal program itself. The cross-compartment trends (ρ ≈ 0.44–0.50, P = 0.06–0.10) did not reach significance with 15 patients; the intra-fibroblast receptor correlation was negative (ρ = −0.71, P = 0.003), and the mixed-effects model showed only weak, directionally unstable cell-level receptor–FAP coupling (slope = 0.05, P = 0.017). SDC4 biology fits this geometry: its ectodomain binds collagen I/III and fibronectin [11], it polarizes toward stroma at the CRC invasive front, and its overexpression carries independent prognostic weight [12]. The decisive tests are spatial (multiplexed immunohistochemistry or spatial transcriptomics demonstrating FAP+ CAF proximity to SDC4/CD44-polarized epithelium) and functional (receptor blockade in co-culture). The senescence analysis (Section 3.11) shows that the FAP-marked program is accompanied by senescence-associated transcriptional features (SenMayo × FAP13 ρ = 0.848/0.770 in TCGA/GSE39582, and 0.756 in the independent cBioPortal entry; CellAge and colon-fibroblast SASP signatures co-vary in the same direction), with MKI67-adjusted robustness and protein-level SASP–ECM covariation (ρ = 0.812, FAP-excluded matrix3). We interpret this as senescence-associated transcriptional covariation, not as demonstrated senescent CAFs: confirming that FAP+ CAFs are senescent, and that SASP drives the matrix phenotype, requires multi-marker protein staining and functional assays (Section 4.6).
[91] 4.3 The stromal transition from barrier to facilitator, and its clinical translation
[92] Histopathology supports a stromal transition in early CRC: stroma can restrain rather than support early tumor growth, as demonstrated experimentally in pancreatic cancer [30, 31], and stroma-rich tumors are consistently associated with adverse outcome in CRC [33]. Our transcriptomic data are consistent with a gradual version of this transition: FAP expression and matrix scores increase monotonically—but modestly and continuously—with T category, without a detectable discrete switch. Whether a sharper threshold exists at the shallow/deep pT1 boundary cannot be resolved with public surgical cohorts, which essentially lack endoscopically resected shallow pT1 specimens.
[93] Two translational directions follow if spatial and functional studies confirm the interface model. First, FAP-targeted imaging could report on stromal activation state. This direction is less speculative than it may appear: Strating et al. showed that FAP immunohistochemistry identifies the stroma-rich CMS4 subtype and that the corresponding tumors can be visualized by [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-PET imaging [27], and FAPI-PET is under translational investigation in CRC peritoneal carcinomatosis, where [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 uptake was used to monitor treatment response [32]. Extension of such readouts to early-lesion risk stratification is untested, but the imaging modality and its link to stromal biology are established. Second, the interface suggests intervention points downstream of FAP—matrix disruption or SDC4/CD44 blockade—at the stage where endoscopic decision-making is binary: for pT1 CRC, current guidelines recommend additional surgery for deep submucosal invasion (≥ 1,000 μm), lymphovascular invasion, poor differentiation, or high-grade budding [10]. Our exploratory nomogram (AUC 0.888, T-stage dominated) and the 80-gene StromalScore's independent nodal association (OR = 3.37) illustrate that transcriptomic stromal-content measures could, in principle, refine borderline decisions—although pathological TSR remains the clinical standard [33], and neither SDC4 nor CD44 has been validated as a therapeutic target in CRC.
[94] Notably, FAP-directed therapies have so far shown limited clinical efficacy despite strong biological rationale [4, 5]. Our data offer a possible explanation: if the relevant effector is the deposited matrix and its epithelial receptors rather than FAP itself, then enzymatic inhibition or depletion of FAP+ cells alone may miss the functional node of the axis. This interpretation remains a hypothesis, but it reframes FAP from a drug target to a spatially informative marker of a druggable stromal state, and it aligns with the emerging view that stroma-modulating strategies need to account for the barrier-versus-facilitator duality of tumor stroma [30, 31]. Whether senescence-associated features within the FAP-marked matrix state represent a targetable axis requires prior functional demonstration that senescent CAFs are present and that SASP contributes to matrix remodelling; no senolytic has been validated in CRC.
[95] 4.4 Discrepant results and open questions
[96] Several discrepancies deserve emphasis rather than smoothing. (i) The bulk receptor-null result versus positive cross-compartment trends: we interpret this as compartment mixing in bulk data, but patient-level confounding is an alternative; spatial data are required. (ii) The cell-level (mixed-effects) weak positive versus patient-level negative receptor–FAP correlation: this layer-of-analysis difference is consistent with within-patient compositional confounding and underscores that receptor co-induction, if present, is weak. (iii) CDH1/E-cadherin mRNA–protein dissociation (null mRNA T-category trends in both cohorts despite protein-level E-cadherin loss reported in the literature). (iv) The T1→T4 FAP–CLDN gradient was directionally opposite between cohorts; we regard both as noise around a null association. (v) dMMR tumors carried higher stromal scores yet a lower nodal metastasis rate—consistent with the immunologically restrained microsatellite instability (MSI) microenvironment [28]—but cross-sectional transcriptomes cannot order these events causally. (vi) The 6-gene ECM proxy failed for nodal risk while the 80-gene StromalScore partially succeeded in TCGA but not GSE39582 (probe coverage), illustrating the sensitivity of transcriptomic TSR proxies to signature completeness and platform. (vii) The SASP–receptor2 correlation was null in TCGA (ρ = 0.036, P = 0.49) and in CPTAC protein data (FAP–receptor ρ = 0.099, P = 0.34) but weakly positive in GSE39582 (ρ = 0.154, P = 2.3 × 10⁻⁴); we report this cross-cohort difference rather than selecting the concordant null, and interpret it cautiously given the mixed-stage composition and platform-specific signal compression of GSE39582.
[97] 4.5 Hypothesis framework
[98] Supported: with increasing local invasion, the FAP-marked stromal/matrix deposition program is progressively enriched; the enrichment is continuous and is not accompanied by receptor co-induction (bulk, single-cell, protein) or by any unified claudin response. The FAP-marked program is additionally enriched for senescence-associated secretory features across two bulk cohorts, with MKI67-adjusted robustness and protein-level SASP–ECM covariation; whether the senescence signal reflects senescent CAFs themselves or a senescence-adjacent transcriptional state requires protein and functional validation. Pan-cancer analyses associating fibroblast-senescence programs with prognosis and immunotherapy response [41] support the translational relevance of this axis, although no such signature has yet been validated for CRC. Candidate, requiring prospective validation: epithelial SDC4/CD44 may sense FAP+ CAF-deposited matrix within specific spatial niches; this candidate derives from patient-level cross-compartment trends, ligand–receptor inference, and SDC4 biology, and is constrained by the bulk, intra-fibroblast, and protein receptor results. Argued against by the present data: unified FAP-to-claudin regulation; FAP-CAF scoring as an independent prognostic marker; stromal SDC4/CD44 as a co-directional component of the FAP/matrix program; and a demonstrable discrete pT1–pT2 molecular switch in public transcriptomic data.
[99] 4.6 Limitations
[100] First, both bulk cohorts contain few early-stage tumors (TCGA Stage I n = 44 with 3 OS events; GSE39582 n = 38 with 5 OS events), shallow pT1 lesions are absent, and the T1 subgroup is tiny (n = 5). Second, ligand–receptor inference is cell-level co-expression and awaits patient-aware reproduction and spatial/functional validation. Third, SDC4 lacks functional characterization in CRC [12]. Fourth, spatial transcriptomic datasets with early-CRC representation were unavailable, and Visium spots mix cells. Fifth, the author-defined FAP-CAF score includes ECM genes, so its association with an ECM program is partly structural; we mitigated this with non-overlapping scores, but residual collinearity cannot be fully excluded. Sixth, the transcriptomic TSR proxy was sensitive to signature completeness (6-gene proxy failed; 80-gene signature partially succeeded in TCGA only); pathological TSR remains the clinical standard [33]. Seventh, MMR stratification was possible only in GSE39582 and the dMMR survival analysis was underpowered. Eighth, the ABSOLUTE purity-adjusted estimate (ρ = 0.906) adjusts for one global copy-number-based purity proxy and does not resolve detailed cellular composition; matched-null analyses are designed to test transcriptomic background but not all possible biological confounders. Ninth, the patient-level single-cell cross-compartment analyses were limited to 15 patients and the trends did not reach significance; the mixed-effects cell-level receptor coupling was weak and directionally unstable, and its P value derives from a t approximation that should be re-confirmed with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom at submission. Tenth, SenMayo and SASP scores quantify senescence-associated transcriptional features, not senescent cells per se; confirmation requires multi-marker protein staining (e.g., p16/p21, SA-β-gal) and functional assays, and bulk scores cannot resolve which cell compartment carries the senescence signal [37, 38]. Eleventh, continuous-score Cox analyses were null for all senescence and matrix scores in both cohorts (all P > 0.10), indicating that the FAP–matrix–senescence axis behaves as a tissue-state marker rather than an independent prognostic marker; this negative result is consistent with the exploratory clinical analyses and does not argue for or against a biological role of the axis.
[101] 4.7 Future directions
[102] Priority experiments: (1) spatial validation of FAP+ CAF proximity to SDC4/CD44-polarized epithelium on early-CRC tissue microarrays including endoscopically resected shallow and deep pT1 lesions; (2) CRC organoid–FAP+ CAF co-culture on defined collagen I/fibronectin matrices with SDC4/CD44 blockade to test receptor dependence, followed by measurement of CLDN1/2/4 abundance, membrane localization, junction continuity, and transepithelial resistance; (3) FAP enzymatic inhibition (e.g., FAPI-04 or talabostat) in the same system; (4) execution of a patient-aware ligand–receptor rebuild in a version-frozen environment; and (5) a prospective early-CRC cohort with pathological TSR, FAP/SDC4/CD44 immunohistochemistry, and long-term follow-up.
[103] 5. Conclusion
[104] FAP marks a progressively enriched stromal matrix program in CRC, reproducible across two independent bulk cohorts and across transcriptomic, proteomic, single-cell, and spatial layers, rather than a regulator of claudin family genes. Matrix enrichment with invasion depth is continuous; no discrete molecular gate is demonstrable in public transcriptomic data. Epithelial SDC4/CD44 emerges as a candidate interface for stroma–epithelium crosstalk—supported by cross-compartment trends, ligand–receptor inference, and receptor uncoupling at the protein level, and constrained by the absence of receptor co-induction in bulk tissue and within fibroblasts. Spatial and functional validation of this interface in early-CRC models is the necessary next step before any gating role can be claimed and before translational applications in imaging or stroma-targeted therapy can be pursued. Finally, the FAP-marked matrix program is enriched for senescence-associated secretory features, linking the stromal program to the senescence-associated secretory phenotype and suggesting that senolytic-informed strategies merit evaluation as upstream interventions.
